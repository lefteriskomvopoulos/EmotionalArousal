# Where the field stands: EDA deconvolution, arousal state estimation, and affect

Working notes for the project proposal. Three sets of work, each paper summarised in at most two
sentences: what it set out to do, and how it did it.

---

## Set A — Deconvolution of electrodermal activity

The problem: skin conductance is the output of slow sweat glands driven by brief sudomotor nerve
bursts, so responses overlap and cannot be read off the trace directly. Every paper below is an
attempt to invert that smearing and recover the underlying neural drive.

**Alexander et al., 2005 — *Journal of Neuroscience Methods* 146:116–123.**
Introduced deconvolution of skin conductance into continuous tonic and phasic signals so that
responses separated by short inter-stimulus intervals could be told apart. The method assumes the
sudomotor nerve signal has a shorter time constant than the measured conductance, which turns
overlapping responses into compact impulses of under two seconds.

**Bach, Daunizeau, Friston & Dolan, 2010 — *Biological Psychology* 85:163–170.**
Proposed a dynamic causal model describing how anticipatory, evoked and spontaneous conductance
changes are generated by sudomotor nerve activity, and inverted it with variational Bayes to infer
the most likely nerve activity behind an observed response. In two fear-conditioning experiments the
model was more sensitive to conditioning effects than conventional response measures.

**Benedek & Kaernbach, 2010 — *Psychophysiology* 47:647–658.**
Developed non-negative deconvolution and continuous decomposition analysis to split skin conductance
into a sparse non-negative driver and a tonic level, forming the basis of the widely used Ledalab
toolbox. Negative parts of the driver are reassigned to a "remainder" signal interpreted as the
additional phasic activity caused by sweat-pore opening.

**Greco, Valenza, Lanata, Scilingo & Citi, 2016 (cvxEDA) — *IEEE TBME* 63:797–804.**
Formulated tonic/phasic separation as a single convex optimisation problem with non-negativity and
sparsity constraints on sudomotor nerve activity. Because the problem is convex the decomposition is
unique and fast to compute, which made it a standard baseline for overlapping responses.

**Amin & Faghih, 2019 — *IEEE Transactions on Biomedical Engineering*.**
Recovered the sparse neural impulses through continuous-time system identification, estimating the
parameters of the sweat-gland system rather than assuming a fixed response shape. This makes the
recovered impulse train specific to the individual whose signal is being analysed.

**Amin & Faghih, 2021 — *IEEE TBME* 68(5):1726–1736.**
Modelled the phasic component with a second-order differential equation representing sweat diffusion
and evaporation, the tonic component with cubic B-splines, and solved for both together. Physiological
priors constrain the system parameters while a sparsity prior keeps the recovered neural stimuli
impulsive, giving a more physiologically grounded decomposition than a fixed biexponential kernel.

**Amin & Faghih, 2022 — *PLOS Computational Biology* 18(7):e1010275.**
Characterised electrodermal activity physiologically so that autonomic activation can be inferred at
near real-time speed. The contribution is computational scalability, which matters for any use of
these methods outside offline laboratory analysis.

**Alam, Amin & Faghih, 2023 — *IEEE OJEMB* 4:234–250.**
Extended sparse decomposition with physiological priors to several simultaneously recorded electrodermal
channels. Sharing one underlying neural drive across channels makes the recovered impulses more robust
to measurement noise and site-specific artefacts.

## Set B — State estimation from the recovered impulses

The problem: once deconvolution returns impulse times and amplitudes, those events are discrete and
noisy, while the quantity of interest — sympathetic arousal — is continuous and hidden. This set
treats the impulses as observations of a latent state and estimates it with Bayesian filtering.

**Wickramasuriya, Qi & Faghih, 2018 — *EMBC*, pp. 3562–3567.**
First formulation in this line: a state-space model that estimates an unobserved stress state from
electrodermal measurements, following earlier state-space work on hidden neural states such as
learning. It establishes the principle that stress need not be classified from labelled data but can
be estimated as a latent variable.

**Wickramasuriya & Faghih, 2019 — *IEEE Transactions on Biomedical Engineering*.**
Built a point-process state-space model in which arousal drives both binary observations (whether a
phasic impulse occurred) and continuous observations derived from the tonic component, with parameters
recovered by expectation–maximisation. Validated on an induced-stress dataset and a fear-conditioning
dataset, it tracks arousal without relying on any external classification labels.

**Wickramasuriya & Faghih, 2020 — *IEEE Access* 8:68499–68513.**
The reference method for this project: a marked point process filter in which the latent arousal state
governs both the rate at which the deconvolved impulses occur and their amplitudes, again with
parameters estimated by expectation–maximisation. It was validated on laboratory stress, real-world
driver stress, Pavlovian fear conditioning and horror-film viewing, and is less sensitive to small
spurious impulses than the earlier binary-and-continuous filter.

**Wickramasuriya & Faghih, 2020 — *PLOS ONE* 15(4):e0231659.**
Combined skin conductance impulses with heart rate in a mixed filter to track sympathetic arousal
during Pavlovian fear conditioning. It shows how a second physiological channel can be folded into the
same state-space framework when one signal alone is not enough.

**Wickramasuriya, Khazaei, Kiani & Faghih, 2023 — *IEEE Access* 11:137204–137247.**
Generalised the framework so that a latent state can be estimated from marked point process and
continuous-valued observations jointly, applied to sympathetic arousal and to cortisol-related energy.
This is the most complete statement of the estimation machinery the project builds on.

**Subramanian, Barbieri & Brown, 2020 — *PNAS* 117(42):26422–26428.**
Showed, independently of the work above, that the timing of electrodermal pulses is well described by
a point process with an inverse-Gaussian inter-pulse distribution, consistent with an integrate-and-fire
mechanism. This supplies the statistical justification for treating the impulses as a point process
rather than as arbitrary noise.

**Subramanian et al., 2021 — *PLOS Computational Biology* 17(6):e1009099.**
Extended that physiological model from pulse timing to pulse amplitude, showing that amplitudes also
follow from the integrate-and-fire account. Together with the 2020 paper it supports the use of a
*marked* point process, in which each event carries an amplitude.

## Set C — The methods applied to emotion, and what electrodermal activity can say about it

### Applications of the framework to affective states

**Faghih et al., 2015 — *EMBC*.**
Characterised fear conditioning and fear extinction through analysis of electrodermal activity, an
early application of this group's modelling to an emotional paradigm. It established the paradigm that
later state-space papers reused for validation.

**Yaghmour, Amin & Faghih, 2021 — *EMBC*, pp. 1055–1060.**
Decoded a cognitive-stress-related arousal state modulated by calming and vexing background music,
using electrodermal activity together with functional near-infrared spectroscopy during a working-memory
task. The motivation is biofeedback: if arousal can be decoded, music can be used to regulate it.

**Khazaei, Amin, Tahir & Faghih, 2024 — *IEEE OJEMB* 5:627–636.**
Used Bayesian filtering within an expectation–maximisation framework to track hidden arousal *and*
cognitive performance during an n-back task performed under calming and exciting music. The aim was to
quantify the arousal–performance link well enough to drive a performance-based arousal decoder.

**Oliver, Zhang, Raju, Murrough & Faghih, 2025 — *PLOS Mental Health* 2(11):e0000463.**
Applied the two-stage pipeline — deconvolution of skin conductance into autonomic activation pulses,
then a marked point process Bayesian filter — to 76 adults receiving 50 auditory, visual and haptic
stimuli rated on the Self-Assessment Manikin scale. The estimated cognitive arousal agreed with
self-ratings better than the raw count of recovered activation events did, and auditory stimuli produced
the highest arousal even though haptic stimuli produced the largest raw responses.

### What electrodermal activity can and cannot distinguish

**Kreibig, 2010 — *Biological Psychology* 84:394–421.**
Reviewed 134 publications on autonomic activity in emotion and found considerable response specificity
once subtypes of emotions are distinguished. Valence-specific patterning proved more consistent than
emotion-specific patterning, with negative emotions producing stronger autonomic responses than positive
ones.

**Behnke, Kreibig, Kaczmarek, Assink & Gross, 2022 — *Emotion Review*.**
Meta-analysed 120 articles, 686 effect sizes and 6,546 participants across eleven positive emotions.
Positive emotions produced no autonomic increase or only weak and highly variable ones, which sets a low
prior on finding clean positive-emotion signatures in any single autonomic channel.

**Behnke, Buchwald, Bykowski, Kupiński & Kaczmarek, 2022 — *Scientific Data* 9:10.**
Published POPANE, the dataset used in this project: 1,157 cases across seven studies, eight biosignals
and a continuous self-report of affect, assembled to study the link between emotional experience and
physiology. The paper documents and validates the recordings; it does not model the sympathetic drive
underlying them.

**Emotion recognition with electrodermal activity, 2025 — *Neurocomputing*.**
Systematic review of electrodermal-activity-based emotion-recognition systems spanning affective science
and affective computing. Arousal prediction models consistently outperform valence prediction models,
which the authors attribute to arousal's direct link to autonomic activity.

## Where this leaves us

Set A has essentially solved the inverse problem: several validated ways exist to recover sudomotor
impulses from skin conductance, from fixed-kernel deconvolution to physiologically constrained sparse
decomposition. Set B has turned those impulses into a principled estimate of a hidden arousal state, and
Set C shows the framework works on stress, fear, sensory stimuli and music.

What is missing sits at the junction of the three. The state-space work has been validated almost
entirely on stress and threat — induced stress, driver stress, fear conditioning, a single horror
clip — in samples of a few to a few dozen participants. It has never been asked whether the state it
recovers can tell emotions apart, and in particular whether it separates negative from positive
emotion or only registers intensity, which is precisely where Kreibig and the Behnke meta-analysis say
the difficulty lies. Meanwhile the emotion literature that does work with many participants and many
emotions, including the POPANE dataset itself, analyses skin conductance through extracted features and
classifiers, reporting which stimulus was shown rather than how the sympathetic drive behind it evolved.
